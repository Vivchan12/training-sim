Insight 01

Digital Transformation and AI Transformation Aren't the Same Job

Plan an AI project like a systems rollout and you'll miss the one risk that matters — until the thing is built and quietly wrong.

24 September 2026

Everyone's calling it "AI transformation" now. Half the time, it's just digital transformation with a different logo on the slide.

That's not a small mix-up. If you plan an AI project like a systems rollout, you'll miss the one risk that actually matters — and you won't find out until the thing is already built and quietly wrong.

Here's where they overlap, and where they don't.

Where they're the same

Most of the process is identical. Strip the "AI" label off, and the skeleton underneath is a transformation project like any other:

SimilarityWhy it applies to both
Start with a business problem, not a toolNeither should start with "let's use X" — both start with what's broken or slow
Need executive sponsorshipBoth stall without a sponsor who can unblock budget and internal politics
Change management is unavoidablePeople resist new workflows whether the tool is a new ERP or a new AI assistant
Success is measured in outcomes, not deployment"We installed it" isn't success in either — adoption and impact are
Phased rollout beats big-bangPilot small, prove value, then scale — standard in both
Data quality is foundationalBad data breaks a CRM migration and breaks an AI model equally
Ongoing support after go-liveNeither is "build and walk away" — both need a support model post-launch

If your AI project is skipping any of these, that's not a special AI problem — it's a basic transformation problem, and it would sink a CRM migration just as easily.

Where they actually diverge

This is the part that gets skipped, and it's the part that costs people.

Digital TransformationAI Transformation
Core skillSystems integration, process re-engineeringPrompt design, model evaluation, data readiness
What "expert" meansKnows the software (SAP, Salesforce, cloud migration)Knows what a model can and can't reliably do
Planning skillRoadmapping, change management, stakeholder alignmentSame, plus judging where AI adds real value vs. hype
Build skillConfiguring and integrating existing systemsBuilding around a probabilistic tool — testing, guardrails, human-in-the-loop design
Risk skillProject and vendor riskModel risk — hallucination, bias, data leakage, over-reliance
Validation skillUAT, sign-off against a specOngoing evaluation — output quality can drift or vary run to run
Org skillTraining staff on new toolsTraining staff to work with an uncertain, changing tool
Time horizonProject has an end dateNever fully done — models and best practice keep moving
Failure modeBudget overrun, adoption failureConfident wrong answers, silent quality decay

The line that matters most is the last one. A failed system migration is loud — it breaks, someone notices, it gets fixed. A failed AI rollout can be quiet. It keeps running. It keeps giving answers. Some of them are just wrong, and nobody's watching for that unless they built the habit of watching in.

The part people skip

Everyone wants to talk about the build. Almost nobody wants to talk about proving the thing works before it's built — because that step can end in "don't build it," and that doesn't look like progress in a status update.

But that's exactly the step that's different about AI transformation. You're not integrating a known system. You're deciding whether to trust a tool that can be confidently wrong, and the only way to know is to test it against real work, with real people, before it's load-bearing.

Skip that, and you haven't done AI transformation. You've done digital transformation with an AI feature bolted on — same discipline, wrong risk model — and the gap doesn't show up until it's expensive.
